Notes on what's happening above, per step:
- Step 1: `createAnalytics({ plugins: [autoPage(), autoUTM(), autoClicks({
  selector: "[data-cta]" }), autoScroll({ thresholds: [25, 50, 100] }),
  autoVisibility()] })` is constructed against a stubbed "page load" whose
  `location.search` carries three of the five standard UTM params
  (`utm_source`/`utm_medium`/`utm_campaign` -- `utm_term`/`utm_content` are
  simply absent from this URL, and so absent from the delivered event too).
- Step 2: both plugins that act immediately at setup have already fired, in
  plugin-array registration order: `autoPage()` first (its unconditional
  initial `.page()` call, representing the page load already in progress),
  then `autoUTM()` (its one-shot "Campaign Landing" event, since UTM params
  are present in the current URL).
- Step 3: a click on `<span>` nested inside the `<a data-cta>` CTA button
  resolves to the CTA itself via `Element.closest("[data-cta]")` --
  `autoClicks()`'s scoping option -- and is tracked with the auto-computed
  element properties. A second click on an unrelated `<a class="nav-link">`
  link (no `data-cta` attribute anywhere in its ancestor chain) produces no
  event at all: `autoClicks({ selector })` silently ignores any click whose
  target doesn't resolve to a match.
- Step 4: three separate `scroll` events, each crossing exactly one
  configured threshold (`[25, 50, 100]`, customized away from the default
  `[25, 50, 75, 100]`) -- each threshold fires exactly once, the moment
  `computeScrollPercent` first reaches or exceeds it.
- Step 5: a single `visibilitychange` to `"hidden"` (e.g. the visitor
  switches tabs) is tracked with the current `document.visibilityState`.
- Step 6: a simulated client-side navigation (`history.pushState`) to
  `"/pricing"` with an empty `location.search`. `autoPage()`'s patched
  `pushState` fires a second `.page()` call for the new route.  `autoUTM()`

  of any kind after setup, so a later navigation is invisible to it by

- Step 7: `analytics.destroy()` tears down every plugin's listeners in

  `[flow] 0 provider call(s) after destroy()` confirms teardown was
  effective end-to-end through the public API, not just at the unit level.
